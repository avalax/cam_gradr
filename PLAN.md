# cam_gradr – design and decisions

How the app works inside and why it was built that way. Using the app is described in [README.md](README.md), working on it in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## 1. Goal

A single-page web app, purely client-side without a backend, that captures a Datacolor **Spyder Checkr Photo** (SCK300) with the webcam and calculates a **3D LUT** that corrects the webcam's color rendition. The correction is visible live in the preview and exported as a `.cube` file, mainly for the Portkeys LH7H camera monitor, which applies the LUT to its HDMI signal.

## 2. User interface

- **Header:** the camera switch (state via `aria-checked`, errors on start next to it) and the gear menu ⚙. The menu is an accordion with at most one section open and a short summary on the right of each closed one: camera (device, resolution as chips, the note to lock the camera's automatic functions), reference values, language and appearance (System, Light, Dark). It closes with Escape, a click outside or the gear.
- **Tabs:** Gray cards (step 1, open first), Color cards (step 2), Preview and Export, freely selectable without locks. Below each name is its result with a mini scale of four bars (`src/ui/tab-status.ts`): Gray cards green when brightness and contrast are both in the normal range, otherwise yellow with the main hint; Color cards by the mean ΔE2000 after the correction, green up to 2, then fewer bars above 2, 4 and 7. Empty without a result.
- **Still images:** Measuring only ever happens on still images, never on the live image. Each tab with a camera image (Gray cards, Color cards, Preview) has its own still image, which is put away when switching and comes back on return. Starting the camera discards only the still image of the active tab, loading a project discards all of them.
- **Gray cards:** a measuring field as tall as the image, brightness vertical (too high at the top) and contrast horizontal (too high on the right), the normal range as a framed window in the middle, the surface in fine gray levels as in the zone system, and a dot for the measurement. The main message sits at the edge towards which the measurement deviates; hover or focus shows the next step and the details (both large patches with target and tolerance, highlights, shadows). The next step is always the brightness first and then the contrast, because both influence each other. There is no histogram.
- **Color cards:** the active reference set, a wipe slider across both cards (camera colors on the left, corrected on the right) and the report (section 5). The correction is recalculated whenever the corner points or the reference set change.
- **Preview:** the camera image with the LUT, the original left of a dividing line that is dragged in the image or moved with the arrow keys, its own button for a still image (a camera icon at the top right of the image while the camera runs, in the same corner toolbar as the trash of the card tabs, so that actions sit on the image and the columns beside it only describe), and a side column: Colors shows the 36 colored patches as target, camera and corrected per card and names the three largest deviations; Grays shows the twelve neutral patches as a wedge, the mean brightening and the channels shifted by at least 1.5 points per tonal range (shadows 0.1–0.3, midtones 0.4–0.6, highlights 0.7–0.9), and the mean deviation before and after. A tone curve diagram was dropped, as it showed almost a straight line for small corrections.
- **Look LUT:** a bar below the preview lays a ready-made 3D LUT (`.cube`, for example a cinema look) over the correction. One list holds "No look LUT", the imported looks and, as its last entry, "+ Import look LUT …" (several files at once); a trash icon appears only next to a chosen look and deletes it with "Undo". The list is a select-only combobox (WAI-ARIA) instead of the browser's select, because the native one reports a change only when its list closes: every arrow step applies the look at once, Escape returns to the look from before, and the import entry runs only on Enter or a click. A slider sets the strength (0–100 %). With a look, the labels above the image are buttons: a click on the left one switches between camera image and correction, a click on the right one between the look over the correction and the look alone on the camera image (the look over the identity). They look like plain labels and only show on hover (pointer, lighter background, a tooltip naming what a click shows) that they react; mockups with a switch or a second toggle in the bar, and with swap arrows next to the names, were dropped. The labels and the hint under the Preview tab name both sides ("Original / look LUT", "Corrected / look LUT", "Original / look LUT only", "Corrected / look LUT only"). The export keeps "Correction only" and "Correction + look LUT"; the look alone is the user's own file already. Messages (imported, rejected with the reason, a warning for conversions from camera log curves recognized by name such as S-Log, LogC or V-Log) stand under the bar. The color patches and the deviation keep showing the correction alone. Import by drag and drop and a management in the gear menu were dropped in the mockups in favor of the one list.
- **Export:** two cards. "LUT for monitor or software" with the content ("Correction only" or "Correction + look LUT", the latter only with a look chosen in the preview and then preset) and the size (17³, 33³ default, 65³) in one row, a line naming the look and its strength with a link to the preview, four slices through the LUT (with the look) as a preview, the estimated file size and the download; without a correction it is grayed out with a link to the Color cards. "Back up project" lists what the file contains and has "Save project" and "Load project" (usable without a correction). The Export tab takes the height of the other tabs.
- **Languages:** English (the reference) and German via vue-i18n with typed keys, texts in `src/lang/<language>/<area>.json`. The browser language decides, the menu can override it; language and appearance are remembered in `localStorage`.

## 3. Capturing the cards

The Spyder Checkr Photo has four cards in a case: two gray cards (18 % and 50 % gray, each with a strip of twelve patches) and two color cards with 48 patches together. One capture always shows a pair, first the gray cards, then the color cards; each card gets its own four corner points. The layouts are `src/data/layouts/*.layout.json` (format `cam_gradr-layout/2`, patch rectangles relative to the card, no color values).

- **Search:** Twice a second the live image is searched for the cards (`src/chart/detect.ts`, without a library), on a copy downscaled to 320 pixels width. Cards are dark areas that do not touch the image edge and stand upright (aspect ratio 0.45 to 0.85); several brightness thresholds are tried, and of the clean candidates the smallest wins. The corners are intersections of four lines fitted to the end pieces of each side (10–30 % of the length from each end), so that the tabs in the middle of the edges and the rounded corners do not interfere. A side that is not straight (blur), or two cards that differ by more than 25 % in size or do not sit side by side, count as not found. Cards are found down to about 12 % of the image height. A first approach via edges failed on a busy background.
- **Edge refinement:** In the downscaled image an edge is only known to one of its pixels; the cards came out 1.5 to 8.5 pixels too small per side at 1280 × 720. Each side is therefore measured again at full resolution, along profiles across the edge, from the darkest point of the frame outward to the first rise and its middle. Only the first rise counts, because the case can cast a gray shadow next to the black plate, the plate's brightness varies, and an overexposed shot blurs the edge into a slow ramp. On synthetic images the corners are within 0.1 pixels at 720p, 1080p and 4K.
- **Capture:** When two consecutive searches find the cards in the same place (within 3 % of the image width), the app averages 8 frames into a still image, searches again on it and switches the camera off. If the cards moved during the averaging, the still image is discarded and the search goes on. The frame around the found cards is white with a dark edge and pulses until they are steady, then draws itself green; there are no texts in the live image. If the colored share of the patches does not fit the step (at least 25 % colored patches means color cards), the frame turns red with "Not color cards" or "Not gray cards", and the cards are not captured again until they have left the image.
- **Corner points** can only be dragged, not placed. The trash icon in the image discards still image and corner points and searches again. Starting the camera also searches again but holds the previous state, which comes back if the camera stops without a new still image (`beginSearch`/`endSearch` in the chart store, `held` in the camera store).
- **Rotation:** Cards held rotated by 180° are measured in both orientations, and the one wins in which the strip (gray cards) or the gray ladder (color cards) runs from dark to bright; in a tie the previous orientation stays. The project stores it as `rotated`.
- **Sampling:** the median of the inner half of each patch, so that edges and reflections drop out.
- **Measured layout:** The rectangles were measured on the stills of the local projects (4 shots per card pair, two webcams): the four edges of each field where the color passes the middle between plate and field, one regular grid per still, and the median over the stills, so that one distorted shot cannot pull it. Both color cards share one grid, and the strip fields of the gray cards have the height and row spacing of the color fields. The margin between the sampled inner half and the real edge of a field is at least 16 % of the planned margin (median 92 %), 11 % when each shot is left out of the fit. Later stills of a third webcam (Dell) confirm the layout: the inner half of every field stays as even there as on the measured stills.

## 4. Step 1: the light

Step 1 sets the room brightness for step 2 and calculates nothing for the LUT. The values are in `DEFAULT_EXPOSURE_CONFIG` (`src/chart/exposure.ts`), assuming an sRGB camera curve:

- **Clipping:** no channel of the brightest strip patch at 99 % or above (about as bright as a white, around 95 % at the target exposure), none of the darkest at 1 % or below. Only real clipping counts; the thresholds were checked on real stills.
- **Brightness** (vertical): the 18 % patch at sRGB 0.46 and the 50 % patch at 0.74, normal range ±0.06, full deflection ±0.3. It shifts both patches equally.
- **Contrast** (horizontal): the distance between the two patches against the target 0.28, normal range ±0.05, full deflection ±0.25. It spreads the patches, so the two controls of a camera can be adjusted separately. The contrast is only displayed; the tone curve makes up for part of it.

The assignment 18 % / 50 % rests on the brightness difference alone, as no label is visible on the cards. The gray cards have no reference values for their strips; the large patches are calculated nominally from the reflectance and serve as an independent check of the correction (section 5).

## 5. Step 2: the correction model

Everything is fitted from the color cards (`src/solve/fit.ts`). Camera values are decoded as sRGB; the references (Lab, D50) are converted to linear sRGB (D65) via Bradford. The color math (Lab, ΔE2000) is tested against colorjs.io.

- **Target:** Matrix, report, gray card check and preview all measure against one target (`targetLab`): the reference at the exposure of the shot, compressed above the shoulder like the tone curves, and kept inside the sRGB gamut like the output. That is the best the correction can show. The neutral patches target their reference color, not pure gray: the gray ladder of the card is slightly warm in both known sources, and a camera that already shows it correctly must stay unchanged.
- **Tone curves:** one monotone curve per channel, which includes the white balance: a smooth polynomial of the gain in log-log space through the twelve neutral patches, with a limited gain that stays constant beyond the measured range, evaluated by PCHIP. Neutral patches that the camera no longer tells apart (slope below 0.1, for example with compressed highlights) are merged into blocks shared by all three channels, as the channels would otherwise sort them differently and produce a color cast; the report names them. A curve whose end would lie above 1 is compressed above the shoulder by one factor, so that the camera's white ends exactly at 1.
- **Matrix:** 3×3 with rows summing to 1 (neutral stays neutral), fitted by least squares with weak regularization towards the identity and then refined for the smallest mean ΔE2000, again with a weak pull towards the identity where the patches do not decide it. The fit keeps at least half of the sRGB luminance of each camera primary, so no camera color turns darker than black. Colors more than 1.3 times as saturated as the reference are penalized, as too much saturation looks worse than too little. The limit was 1.1 up to version 1.7: as a 3×3 matrix cannot take back the saturation of one hue alone, the Sony's over-saturated blues (6F by 29 %) then made its reds and yellows dull too, and the eight most saturated patches reached only 78 to 88 % of the target. With 1.3 the mean error dropped on all eight local shots (Sony 4.11 → 3.99, second Sony 5.14 → 4.80), while the mean saturation of the color patches stays at 98 to 104 % of the target; without a limit the washed-out third Lenovo shot ends up 13 % above it.
- **Hue table:** after the matrix, a hue rotation and a chroma factor per hue, as smooth functions around the hue circle: a Fourier series up to the second harmonic, ten numbers. They are fitted for the smallest mean ΔE2000 with the same saturation penalty and a strong pull towards no change (5 per squared coefficient), so the table only follows what several patches of a hue agree on. It keeps luminance and grays and scales each hue ray as a whole, so the most saturated scene colors beyond the card are not extrapolated. Camera profiles work the same way: DNG profiles have a hue/saturation table on top of their matrix, DCamProf and Lumariver relax such a table towards the matrix and leave lightness alone by default, and Datacolor's SpyderCheckr software writes its calibration as hue, saturation and brightness per color band. The extra patches of the 48-patch card fill the inside of the gamut (skin tones, medium saturation RGBCMY, near white and black, a finer gray ramp), which is where such a table needs support. Compared with the matrix alone on the eight local shots (6-fold cross-check of the color patches, each fold fitted from scratch), the error of the left-out patches fell on seven of them (Sony 4.89 → 4.41, second to fourth Sony 5.85 → 5.52, 4.09 → 3.66, 4.13 → 3.63, Lenovo up to −0.2) and rose slightly on the Dell (6.31 → 6.41). On the stills at most 0.13 % of the pixels differ by more than 10 ΔE76 from the matrix alone, and nothing is clipped. The table turns hues by at most about 7° and changes chroma by at most about 9 %. Projects from before version 1.8 have no table and keep their correction.
- **Gamut:** The camera colors on the way from a gray to full saturation (a channel at 0) end up on one ray from the gray of the same luminance. Where the end of that ray would lie beyond the sRGB cube, the part beyond 80 % of the way to the edge is compressed by one factor, so that the end lands exactly on the edge. Colors keep their order and their proportions, only a fully saturated camera color reaches the edge, and directions that fit stay as they are (a camera that is already right keeps its pure yellow at 255/255).
- **White:** camera values close to clipping in all channels (darkest channel above 0.96) are blended towards white, so that a clipped white stays white instead of taking on the white balance as a color. Per channel this is not possible, because the channels of a camera clip at different brightnesses.
- **Report:** ΔE2000 per patch before and after, mean and maximum, the mean separately for the neutral patches (tone curves) and the colored ones (matrix and hue table), and the check with the large gray patches G50 and G18 at the exposure of the color shot. From ΔE2000 3 after the correction it warns that the light or the camera's automatic functions may have changed between the captures. The gray cards stay out of the fit: there are no measured targets for them, and they come from another capture.

Measured on twelve stills of the local projects (three cameras: Lenovo, Sony, Dell), no pixel is clipped (before version 1.7 up to 1.5 % of all pixels and 8 % of the saturated ones), and the mean error of the color patches stayed about the same (Dell 6.2 → 6.0). Patches next to the edge of sRGB, such as the cyan 1F, deviate a little more, as the old code met them only by clipping.

**Tone correction always on:** Up to version 1.6 a checkbox could switch the tone curves off, leaving one white-balance factor per channel. Compared on six shots of three cameras, that was worse in all of them for the neutral patches (by 1.5 to 2.8 ΔE2000), left a visible cast on the gray ramp, and made the Dell image dark and too contrasty. The project file no longer has the `tone` field.

**Root-polynomial terms (tried, rejected):** PR #63 added the three root terms (√r − √g)², (√g − √b)² and (√r − √b)² to the matrix, chosen by a cross-check over 6 groups of patches and capped to the color range of the patches. On the patches they helped clearly (second Sony still 6.8 → 5.2 ΔE2000 for left-out patches, Dell 7.3 → 5.5), but on the real stills a red sweater turned crimson and darker (L 37 → 21, hue 35° → 15°; the matrix keeps the real 37 and 33°), and 15 % to 88 % of the saturated pixels were pushed out of sRGB with green at 0. Lessons: patch errors, even cross-checked, do not show what a model does to scene colors beyond the card, so render the stills in the project files before trusting a model change; and a cross-check must fit each part from scratch, as starting from the fit with all patches leaks the left-out ones. Re-tested for version 1.8 with the saturation limit and the gamut compression: nothing is clipped any more, and the left-out patches improve on average from 5.46 to 4.95, but 7 % to 15 % of the pixels of the Sony and Dell stills still differ by more than 10 ΔE76 from the matrix alone, the sweater turns wine red again and the Dell's blue lamp almost black. Polynomial terms of second order are unstable (one left-out Dell patch at 35). Mackiewicz, Andersen and Finlayson (JOSA A 33, 2016) describe exactly this: polynomial and root-polynomial mappings "will distort the hue planes, which can lead to … hue shifts and possibly unstable predictions of very saturated colors".

## 6. LUT, preview and export

- **LUT** (`src/lut`): the model evaluated on an N³ grid of sRGB code values, red fastest, values 0..1. The correction always applies in full; an early strength slider was dropped.
- **Preview** (`src/gl`): a 33³ LUT as an 8-bit RGBA 3D texture (runs everywhere, also with software GL), sampled trilinearly by a lean WebGL2 fragment shader in real time. `sampleLut` computes the same interpolation in TypeScript for the tests. Without WebGL2 the LUT is applied on the CPU, downscaled to at most 480 pixels width, and the tab says so.
- **Look LUT** (`src/look`): the `.cube` reader takes 3D LUTs of size 2 to 129 with `DOMAIN_MIN`/`DOMAIN_MAX` or `LUT_3D_INPUT_RANGE`, skips `TITLE` and unknown keywords, and rejects 1D LUTs, a missing size, broken numbers, a wrong number of value lines and an empty input range with the line. The look is named after its file, as the `TITLE` of look files is often missing or only names the program. Combined per grid point: the correction exactly, then the look (trilinear, scaled to its input range), mixed linearly by the strength, out = c + s · (look(c) − c). Because sampling interpolates linearly, the mixed LUT gives the mixed image. The preview keeps the correction and the full look as 33³ LUTs and only mixes them on the slider, so it follows without delay; the left side gets a second LUT texture (the correction) or the original. Looks are stored in IndexedDB like the reference sets, in their own object store `looks` (database version 2; the upgrade keeps the reference sets). Tested with five commercial look LUTs (33³, Rec.709 input, after a conversion LUT in their own workflow), which stay in `local-data/`.
- **Export:** `cam_gradr-<size>.cube` (with a look `cam_gradr-<size>-<look>-<strength>.cube`) with a comment naming the app version and, with a look, one naming the look and its strength, `TITLE`, `LUT_3D_SIZE`, `DOMAIN_MIN` and `DOMAIN_MAX`, decimal point and `\n` regardless of system settings. The tests read the file back and check it against the model. Other formats (`.3dl`, Hald PNG) are not planned.

## 7. Projects and reference sets

- **Project file** (`cam_gradr-project/1`, `src/project`): corner points, measurements, model (tone curves, matrix and, since version 1.8, the hue table), preview settings (divider and, if chosen, the look LUT by name and strength), `rotated` and the name of the reference set, never its values. With "Include still images" (default on), the still image of each tab goes into the optional field `stills` (RGB with 8 bits per channel, deflate, Base64; size does not matter, as nothing goes over a wire). With "Include look LUTs" (checked by default, shown as soon as a look LUT is imported), all imported look LUTs go into the optional field `looks` (name, size, input range and the values as 32-bit floats, little-endian, deflate, Base64, so they come back exactly). Corner points belong to a still image and are only saved with it. Loading validates strictly (format, cards, patches, value ranges, model), changes nothing on a faulty file, restores corner points and still images and switches the camera off. With the reference set available the report is recalculated, otherwise the saved model still serves preview and export. Look LUTs from the file that this browser does not have are taken over and saved; one of the same name in the browser stays. A chosen look LUT that neither the browser nor the file has gets a notice; until it is imported, preview and export show the correction alone.
- **Saving to the loaded file** (`src/project/project-file.ts`): Where the browser has the File System Access API (Chrome, Edge), loading and saving use the system dialogs, and the save dialog opens in the folder of the last file with its name preselected. Firefox keeps the file input and downloads under the last name. The file is remembered until the page reloads; closing a dialog without a choice is no error.
- **Reference sets** (`src/reference`): The bundled set (`src/data/reference/spyder-checkr-48.reference.json`) is always loaded and preselected and cannot be deleted; an own set of the same name takes precedence. Own sets come as JSON (`cam_gradr-reference/1`) or CSV, are validated (known IDs, value ranges, white point D50, at least 12 patches with 4 neutral ones) and stored only in IndexedDB (`avalax.cam_gradr`). Patches without a reference value stay out of the calculation; without any set the calculation is locked. Fetching a set from an internet address was deliberately left out.

## 8. Architecture

```text
src/
  camera/     camera access behind the thin adapter CameraApi, frame averaging, still images
  chart/      layouts, card search, homography, patch sampling, card kind, step 1 assessment
  color/      sRGB, XYZ, Lab, Bradford, ΔE2000
  solve/      tone curves, matrix, hue table, gamut handling, error report
  lut/        3D LUT, .cube export, preview settings
  look/       look LUTs: .cube reader, combination with the correction, store
  gl/         WebGL2 renderer and CPU fallback
  project/    project file, still image codec, file dialogs
  reference/  reference sets, validation
  storage/    IndexedDB for reference sets and look LUTs
  lang/       texts per language and area
  theme/      appearance
  ui/         components, tab status and the stored choices
  data/       layouts and the bundled reference set
```

Data flow: camera → still image → (step 1) gray measurement → light assessment only → (step 2) color measurement → fit → 3D LUT (→ look LUT at its strength) → WebGL texture (preview) and `.cube` file (export).

Stack: Vue 3 (`<script setup>`), Pinia, TypeScript (strict), Vite, Tailwind CSS, vue-i18n; no runtime libraries for math or image analysis. TypeScript stays at `~6.0` because `typescript-eslint` does not support TypeScript 7 yet.

**Testing:** Camera, WebGL and canvas sit behind thin adapters that are tested with fakes, so they count towards the 100 % coverage; GLSL is not counted, its math is mirrored by `sampleLut`. Test images are generated in the tests. Unit tests run without a DOM unless a file asks for happy-dom; a lint rule instead of English text tests makes sure that no text bypasses `src/lang/`. The tests follow the test pyramid. The rating, the formats and the edge cases are tested in unit and component tests. The acceptance tests (`e2e/`) cover one journey per user story plus what only a real browser shows: layout, the camera stream, WebGL pixels, downloads, storage across a reload and hover styles. They replace `getUserMedia` with a canvas stream on which the cards are drawn, because Firefox cannot use a video file as a camera. The stream shows gray cards in a well lit room, or color cards with a known distortion and made-up reference values. The tests assert the German interface (the default browser locale is `de-DE`); `settings.spec.ts` covers the switch. The same canvas camera renders the picture in the README (`npm run readme:image`, `e2e/readme/`, not part of the acceptance tests): the real app measures the bundled reference values seen through a webcam with a warm, slightly green cast, calculates the correction and shows a drawn room in the preview, in English and light.

**Pipeline:** The CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs `verify` (commit messages of pull requests, actionlint, lint, format, typecheck, knip, `npm audit` for production dependencies, unit tests with coverage, build) and `acceptance` (Playwright, one job per browser side by side) on every push and pull request. Chrome comes with the runner image and Firefox is Playwright's own build; the system packages of `playwright install --with-deps` are left out, as the tests do not need them and their download from the Ubuntu mirror took about a minute and once hung for more than ten. Every job stops after 15 minutes. `node_modules` is cached per lockfile and shared with the preview and release workflows (`.github/actions/setup`), so `npm ci` only runs when the lockfile changes. A new push to a pull request cancels its outdated run; on `main` a running run is never cancelled, only a waiting one gives way to a newer commit. CodeQL and Dependabot (npm and GitHub Actions) run alongside. The release workflow runs release-please and attaches the build of a release as a zip. `pr-preview.yml` builds every pull request under `pr-preview/pr-<number>/` and keeps the builds on the branch `pr-previews`, as one commit so that it does not grow; a push that loses the race against another pull request is rejected by its lease and starts again. The Pages workflow publishes the zip of the latest release at the root together with the previews. It runs via `workflow_run` after the release workflow and after every preview change, so always from `main`, because only `main` may deploy to the `github-pages` environment.

## 9. Decisions

- **Reference values:** The bundled set is an unofficial transcription from a blog post (bartneck.de), named as such in the UI, the README and the file; no manufacturer data is shipped. Tests use made-up references; the bundled set is only checked for validity. Own shots and files live in `local-data/` (ignored).
- **Device:** The app only writes the LUT file; it is copied to the monitor by hand, without any device integration. Which size the monitor accepts is tested by the user.
- **Camera automatics:** only a warning. The user locks auto exposure and auto white balance in the camera software; the app sets no constraints.
- **Version:** `APP_VERSION` in `src/ui/app-version.ts` takes the version from `package.json`, which release-please raises; only that field ends up in the bundle. The footer and the first line of the `.cube` file show it, and a preview of a pull request adds its number, as it contains unreleased changes.
- **Color space:** sRGB / Rec.709 for input and output. The LH7H applies the LUT to an SDR HDMI signal in Rec.709, so a wider output space (P3, Rec.2020) would only help if the whole chain carried it.
- **License:** MIT, Copyright (c) 2026 Avalax, for the source code; the bundled reference set is not covered.
- **Application identifier:** `avalax.cam_gradr` (`APP_ID` in `src/ui/app-info.ts`), shown in the footer, the prefix of the keys in `localStorage` and the name of the IndexedDB database.

## 10. Risks

- **Dynamic range:** 8-bit video and the camera's own tone mapping limit the accuracy. The LUT only fits the locked camera state; after a change of light or settings, capture again.
- **Reference accuracy:** An unofficial transcription can contain errors, and a real card can deviate (batch, aging). The app validates format and value ranges, not correctness.
- **Decoding:** The LUT is calculated from the image as the browser decodes it. A device that decodes the signal differently (BT.601 instead of BT.709, limited instead of full range) gets a LUT that does not fit exactly.
- **Copyright:** The owner decided to ship the transcription with its source named; the UI makes clear that it is not a manufacturer file.
